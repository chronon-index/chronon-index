# Pricing life-years in public — the ethics memo (RP Part V Q5)

*Drafted 2026-09-09; the pre-written response to the criticism that
WILL come. Not reactive PR copy — the positions here are structural
facts of the design, written down before launch so the answer under
fire is the answer we already gave.*

## The criticism, stated at full strength

"You have put a dollar price on human life. A trader can now profit
when people die sooner; a pension fund can hedge against people
living. This commodifies existence itself."

## The response, in five load-bearing parts

1. **Society already prices life-years — covertly.** Every regulator
   uses a Value of Statistical Life (~$10-13M in current US agency
   practice) to decide which safety rules are "worth it"; every health
   system uses QALY thresholds (NICE's ~£20-30k/QALY) to decide which
   treatments to fund; every court awards wrongful-death damages.
   The choice is not priced-vs-unpriced; it is transparent-vs-buried.
   SAECULUM's number is computed in the open from public statistics by
   code anyone can run — the *opposite* of the actuarial black boxes
   that already govern these decisions. (Anchor figures (verify) at
   publication; the argument does not depend on their exact values.)
2. **The index prices the aggregate, never a person.** S is a sum over
   eight billion lives; the manipulation paper shows even 100,000
   deaths move it by parts per million. No individual's death is
   legible in the number, no individual can be traded, and the
   personal page computes a visitor's own estimate strictly client-side
   — the pipeline cannot see a person even in principle.
3. **The payoff structure rewards flourishing.** Supply grows when
   births rise and when mortality falls; every holder is structurally
   LONG humanity's time. The 1000-event analysis makes it precise: the
   upside tail belongs entirely to longevity breakthroughs and births;
   death shocks are bounded and mean-revert. An instrument that makes
   the world's default portfolio long human life corrects an
   asymmetry — today, nobody's balance sheet gains when everyone
   lives longer, and several (annuity writers) lose.
4. **The hedgers are the good guys of this story.** A pension fund
   hedging longevity is protecting pensioners from outliving its
   solvency; an insurer on the other side is matching a liability it
   already carries. These trades exist today in opaque bilateral
   swaps; an open index moves them into daylight.
5. **What we will not do — in writing.** No instrument on any
   individual's or identifiable group's mortality, ever (world
   aggregate only); no marketing that frames death as upside; the
   personal page's "population statistics, not prophecy — not the
   price of your life" framing is a permanent design rule (RP P7's
   ethics gate governs anything deeper); and issuance equity for any
   future personalized layer is explicitly flagged as political
   philosophy requiring the Rawls/luck-egalitarianism reading (Q6) —
   not something an engineer decides by default.

## Comms discipline

The one-sentence answer, for every interview: "Governments already
price life-years behind closed doors to decide what your safety and
your medicine are worth — we compute one number for all of humanity,
in the open, from public statistics, and anyone can check it."
Never lead with the token; lead with the measurement. Never say
"bet on death" is impossible — say it is *unprofitable by
construction* and show the math.
